# Customer Operating System

## Thesis

The future of lifecycle is not just better messaging. It is a connected customer experience that knows who the customer is, what they bought, what they are trying to achieve, and what action is most useful next.

## Core Experience

A useful subscriber-first digital experience can bring together:
- onboarding
- product education
- usage / habit support
- progress checkpoints
- subscription management
- loyalty
- reviews
- personalized cross-sell
- customer support
- health / preference data where appropriate

## Recommended Experience Layers

### Home / My Account
Show the most relevant next action, not a generic dashboard.

### Onboarding
Capture zero-party data and use it to personalize the experience.

### Habit Support
Support consistency through reminders, streaks, recovery messaging, and routine-building.

### Progress
Use lifecycle checkpoints to help customers interpret whether the product is working and what to expect next.

### Subscription Controls
Make pause, skip, change date, change product, and other management actions easy to find.

### Loyalty
Surface milestone rewards, recognition, and benefits in the same place customers manage the relationship.

### Expansion
Recommend products based on known customer state, goals, and ownership rather than generic merchandising.

## Architecture Principle

Do not create a second disconnected customer database just to power the experience.

Use a durable customer ID and a defined customer state model so the experience can sit on top of the existing commerce, subscription, messaging, and data stack.

## Build Principle

Start with the highest-value customer jobs before building a giant feature set.

A strong V1 should solve:
- what should I do next?
- am I using this correctly?
- what progress should I expect?
- when is my next order?
- how do I change it?
- what benefits do I have?

## Principle

The digital experience should make lifecycle feel like a product, not a sequence of messages.
