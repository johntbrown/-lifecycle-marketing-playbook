# Retention Framework

## Objective

Retention is the work of keeping value, expectations, usage, and subscription fit aligned over time.

Churn is rarely one problem. It is usually the result of a breakdown in one or more of these areas:

- customer readiness
- habit / adherence
- perceived progress
- value confidence
- product fit
- cadence / inventory fit
- billing expectations
- customer support or operational friction

## The Retention System

### 1. Reinforce value before the rebill
Do not wait until cancellation intent appears. Use the period before renewal to remind the customer what progress looks like and why continuing matters.

### 2. Make subscription fit adjustable
Customers should be able to right-size cadence, pause, skip, swap, or change product before canceling.

### 3. Separate habit problems from value problems
A customer who forgets to use the product needs a different intervention from a customer who uses it consistently but does not believe it is working.

### 4. Create proof
Use progress milestones, customer feedback, education, product usage, and objective outcomes where appropriate to reinforce value.

### 5. Treat loyalty as a retention mechanic
Loyalty should increase the perceived value of staying, not simply distribute discounts.

## Retention Questions

- Where are customers dropping?
- What changed immediately before the drop?
- Is the problem usage, value, price, cadence, product fit, or operations?
- What intervention can happen before cancellation intent?
- What is the incremental retention and economic impact?

## Success

Retention should be evaluated through cohort and customer economics, not only channel engagement.

Useful outcomes include:

- rebill rate
- order progression
- active subscription rate
- voluntary / involuntary churn
- retained save rate
- lifetime revenue / LTV
- expansion and product adoption
