# Resilient Adherence

## Thesis

Habit-building programs often fail because they treat consistency as perfection.

For recurring products, the better goal is resilient adherence: help customers recover quickly after a missed day instead of interpreting one miss as failure.

## Why It Matters

A rigid streak can create an all-or-nothing mindset:
- miss once
- feel behind
- product starts to accumulate
- usage declines further
- subscription feels wasteful
- cancellation becomes more likely

## Better Messaging

Reinforce recovery instead of guilt.

Examples of the principle:
- missing one day does not erase progress
- restart today rather than waiting for Monday
- attach the behavior to an existing routine
- reduce the minimum action when motivation is low

## Useful Lifecycle Tactics

- missed-day recovery prompts
- weekly consistency instead of perfect streaks
- routine-stacking education
- visible progress checkpoints
- storage / placement recommendations
- travel continuity support
- usage check-ins that trigger help before backlog develops

## Behavioral Segmentation

A simple maturity model:

### Automatic
The behavior is embedded and requires little conscious effort.

### Mostly Consistent
Occasional misses, but routine is stable.

### Fragile
Customer is trying but frequently forgets.

### At Risk
Usage is inconsistent enough that product buildup or cancellation is likely.

Each state should receive different support.

## Measurement

Look beyond engagement:
- usage consistency
- product buildup signals
- skip / delay behavior
- first and second rebill
- retained subscription
- recovery after missed usage

## Principle

Retention improves when the system makes it easy to come back after imperfect behavior. Design for recovery, not guilt.
