# Cancellation and Save Strategy

## Thesis

A cancellation flow should diagnose the problem before offering a discount.

The customer is telling you why the relationship is breaking. Use that signal to solve the actual issue.

## Reason-Based Treatment

Common cancellation reasons require different interventions:

### Too Much Product / Cadence Mismatch
- skip
- pause
- delay next order
- reduce frequency
- change quantity

### Too Expensive
- lower-cost configuration
- frequency adjustment
- product right-sizing
- targeted save offer only when needed

### Not Seeing Results
- expectation reset
- usage education
- progress framework
- objective proof / measurement
- product-fit check

### Forgot / Not Using Consistently
- habit support
- reminder strategy
- routine stacking
- pause instead of cancel

### Wrong Product / Preference
- product swap
- alternative recommendation
- channel or format change

### Health / Allergy / Safety
- route to support or the appropriate expert workflow
- do not solve sensitive health concerns with generic marketing automation

## Save Hierarchy

A useful order of operations:

1. Solve the operational problem.
2. Adjust the subscription.
3. Change the product or plan.
4. Add education / expectation support.
5. Use a financial incentive when it is the right tool.

This reduces discount dependency and produces more durable saves.

## Measurement

Do not use immediate save acceptance as the only metric.

Track:
- retained save rate
- next successful rebill
- 30 / 60 / 90-day retained status
- repeat cancellation
- discount dependency
- margin impact

A save is only valuable if the customer actually stays.

## Cancellation Data As Customer Intelligence

Cancellation reasons should feed back into:
- acquisition messaging
- onboarding
- product education
- pricing / packaging
- subscription cadence
- product development

The cancellation flow is not just a retention tool. It is one of the best structured customer-feedback systems a subscription business has.
