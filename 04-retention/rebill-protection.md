# Rebill Protection

## Thesis

Subscription churn is often created before the customer hits cancel. The best retention work reduces the reasons to cancel before the billing event arrives.

## Core Idea

Rebill protection should answer four questions before the next charge:

1. Does the customer understand the value they have received so far?
2. Are they using the product consistently enough to judge it fairly?
3. Does the current shipment cadence fit their actual consumption?
4. Do they know how to manage the subscription without leaving?

## The Save Window

The highest-leverage work often happens in the days leading into a rebill, not after a cancellation request.

Useful interventions:
- progress reminders
- expectation setting
- inventory checks
- usage / adherence prompts
- skip / pause / change-date options
- frequency changes
- product swaps
- lower-cost configurations
- shipment reminders
- value framing

Operational saves should come before discounts whenever possible.

## Billing Reminder Strategy

Billing reminders are not just transactional compliance messages. They are also part of the retention experience.

A strong reminder can:
- reduce surprise
- reinforce what is coming
- surface subscription controls
- redirect problems before charge
- reduce refunds and support contacts

If testing reminders, use a clean holdout where legally appropriate and measure more than conversion.

## Measurement

Primary:
- rebill completion
- cancellation rate
- skip / pause rate
- retained subscription rate

Secondary:
- refunds
- CX contacts
- dunning entry
- dunning recovery
- downstream retention
- discount usage

## Principle

The goal is not to stop every cancellation. The goal is to make sure customers who still have product-market fit are not churning because of preventable friction, timing, or surprise.
