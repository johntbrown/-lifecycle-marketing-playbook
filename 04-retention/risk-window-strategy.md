# Retention Risk Windows

## Thesis

Retention work becomes much clearer when it is organized around customer risk windows instead of generic calendar milestones.

The question is not “what should happen on day 30?” The question is “what decision is the customer making next, and what information or support do they need before that moment?”

## Risk Window 1: First Rebill

Customer question:
> Do I want another order?

Primary risks:
- unclear expectations
- inconsistent usage
- product buildup
- surprise billing
- weak perceived value

Useful interventions:
- value reinforcement
- expectation setting
- progress check
- inventory / cadence check
- manage-subscription education
- right-size quantity or timing

## Risk Window 2: Second Rebill

Customer question:
> Is this actually working well enough to keep paying for?

Primary risks:
- no clear proof of progress
- early enthusiasm wearing off
- routine instability
- growing inventory mismatch
- increasing price sensitivity

Useful interventions:
- progress validation
- customer-specific proof
- review / testimonial reinforcement
- usage and routine reinforcement
- cadence optimization
- milestone recognition

## Risk Window 3: Proof-or-Lose-Them

Customer question:
> Have I gotten enough value to make this part of my life?

Primary risks:
- subjective results are unclear
- benefits are difficult to attribute
- product feels optional
- value has not compounded

Useful interventions:
- objective measurement where appropriate
- progress recap
- benefit education
- loyalty milestone
- personalized expansion
- long-term expectation framing

## Post-Cancel Recovery

Customer question:
> Did I leave because the product was wrong, or because the experience was wrong?

Use cancellation reason, product ownership, timing, recency, and prior value to determine the return path.

## Active Subscriber Expansion

Customer question:
> What is the next most relevant thing for me?

Use known customer state, goals, product ownership, and behavior to drive:
- cross-sell
- upsell
- referrals
- reviews
- loyalty
- education

## Principle

Lifecycle strategy should be anchored to customer decisions and risk moments, not arbitrary communication calendars.
