# Loyalty as a Retention System

## Thesis

Loyalty should not begin with points. It should begin with the behaviors and moments that make a customer more likely to stay.

A strong loyalty program can reinforce:
- continued subscription
- product usage
- milestone completion
- cross-sell
- referrals
- reviews
- education
- community participation

## Start With The Business Question

Before designing mechanics, ask:
- Which behavior are we trying to increase?
- At what lifecycle moment does the reward matter?
- Is the reward incremental or simply expensive generosity?
- Does it improve retention enough to cover its cost?

## Reward Design

Useful dimensions to test:
- cash vs physical reward
- universal vs redemption-based reward
- surprise-and-delight vs predictable milestone
- points vs no points
- product reward vs discount

## Manual vs Automatic Redemption

Manual redemption:
- lower cost
- creates engagement signal
- may suppress participation

Automatic reward:
- lower friction
- higher reach
- more expensive
- harder to separate reward impact from delivery cost

## Measurement

Do not judge loyalty on redemption rate alone.

Primary business outcomes can include:
- incremental rebill
- third-order completion
- retained subscription
- net LTV after reward cost
- second-product attachment
- referral behavior

Redemption is useful, but it is a mechanism metric.

## Experimental Design

When testing loyalty:
- maintain a clean control
- use durable cohort assignment
- avoid mid-test treatment contamination
- measure assignment-level impact first
- measure redeemer behavior second
- include reward cost in the decision

## Principle

The best loyalty program creates a reason to continue the relationship. The reward should reinforce customer value, not hide a weak customer experience.
