# Behavioral Risk Segmentation

## Thesis

Retention segmentation should respond to behavior before the customer reaches the cancellation page.

The strongest signals often come from what customers stop doing, delay doing, or repeatedly change.

## Useful Risk Signals

Examples:
- missed usage check-ins
- declining engagement
- repeated skip or delay behavior
- product buildup
- pause history
- failed payment
- support contacts
- lack of progress confirmation
- low product usage confidence
- repeated subscription-management actions

## Example Risk Segments

### Healthy / On Track
Consistent usage, normal cadence, no major friction.

Action:
- reinforce progress
- milestone recognition
- expansion when relevant

### Habit Risk
Customer intends to continue but usage is inconsistent.

Action:
- routine support
- missed-day recovery
- lower-friction reminders

### Cadence Risk
Customer is using the product but shipment timing is mismatched.

Action:
- delay
- frequency change
- quantity adjustment

### Value / Proof Risk
Customer is engaged but unsure whether the product is working.

Action:
- expectation reset
- progress evidence
- objective proof where appropriate
- product-fit support

### Price Risk
Customer signals value sensitivity.

Action:
- right-size first
- lower-cost plan or cadence
- discount only when necessary

### Dunning Risk
Payment failure or card issue.

Action:
- payment recovery
- account access
- billing clarity

## Risk Segmentation Rule

Do not treat every risk signal as a reason to send more marketing.

Some customers need:
- less contact
- a product change
- a cadence change
- customer support
- a payment fix

## Principle

Retention segmentation should identify the most likely reason the relationship could fail, then route the customer to the most appropriate intervention.
