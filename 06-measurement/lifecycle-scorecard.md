# Lifecycle Scorecard

## Thesis

Lifecycle performance should be judged by customer behavior and business outcomes, not by opens and clicks alone.

## Acquisition
- lead-to-first-order conversion
- first-time customer conversion
- new subscription conversion
- revenue per lead
- time to conversion
- downstream repeat rate by source

## Onboarding
- onboarding completion
- early usage / habit indicators
- first rebill completion
- early cancellation
- skip / pause / order-date changes

## Retention
- cohort retention by lifecycle milestone
- first, second, and third rebill
- retained subscription rate
- voluntary churn
- involuntary churn
- dunning recovery

## Expansion
- second-product attach
- upsell / cross-sell conversion
- average products per customer
- expansion LTV

## Recovery
- paused-to-active
- canceled-to-active
- lapsed purchaser reactivation
- next-order completion after reactivation
- repeat churn after save / winback

## Loyalty
- milestone participation
- redemption
- incremental rebill
- referral rate
- LTV after reward cost

## Guardrails

Every growth metric should be paired with relevant guardrails:
- unsubscribe
- spam complaint
- refund
- support contact rate
- margin
- discount dependency
- deliverability
- customer satisfaction

## Cohort Discipline

Whenever possible, read lifecycle performance by:
- acquisition cohort
- product
- plan / pack
- acquisition source
- subscription vs one-time
- customer state

Blended numbers can hide mix shifts. A falling blended retention rate does not automatically mean the underlying subscriber experience deteriorated.

## Principle

A lifecycle scorecard should tell leadership whether customer relationships are getting stronger, not simply whether marketing messages were opened.
