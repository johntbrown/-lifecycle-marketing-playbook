# Zero-Party Data Segmentation

## Thesis

The most useful zero-party data is information that changes the customer experience.

Do not collect quiz answers simply because they are interesting. Collect what helps determine message, product, cadence, support, or next-best action.

## High-Value Questions

Useful categories include:
- primary goal
- reason for purchase
- recent trigger or event
- current routine
- expected outcome
- confidence / readiness
- support preference
- product concerns
- objective measurement availability

## How To Use It

### Acquisition
Use stated goals and need-state to tailor:
- hero message
- proof
- product recommendation
- objection handling

### Onboarding
Use readiness and routine data to tailor:
- usage guidance
- habit support
- expectation setting
- educational content

### Retention
Use progress and challenge data to tailor:
- proof reinforcement
- cadence changes
- support
- value messaging
- save treatment

### Expansion
Use product ownership and goals to prioritize the next most relevant product or service.

## Example Segments

### Goal-Driven
Customer has a specific measurable outcome in mind.

### General Wellness
Customer wants to feel healthier but has a less concrete target.

### Routine-Ready
Customer already has a stable health routine and can easily attach the product.

### Routine-Fragile
Customer is motivated but lacks a consistent habit.

### Proof-Seeking
Customer needs objective evidence before committing long term.

## Governance

For every zero-party field, define:
- why it is collected
- where it is stored
- whether it should persist
- what experience it changes
- whether it is safe and appropriate to use in marketing

## Principle

Ask fewer questions, but make the answers matter.
