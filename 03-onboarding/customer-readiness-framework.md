# Customer Readiness Framework

## Thesis

Most early retention problems are not caused by one bad email or one bad offer. They usually come from some combination of customer readiness and subscription fit.

A customer is more likely to retain when they:
- understand what they bought
- know how and when to use it
- know what progress should look like
- have a routine for using it consistently
- understand the subscription cadence
- know how to manage that subscription
- have enough confidence in the value to accept the next charge

## Onboarding Job To Be Done

Onboarding should move customers through three stages:

### 1. Start Strong
Help the customer understand the product and remove immediate friction.

Questions to answer:
- What did I buy?
- How do I use it?
- Where should I keep it?
- What happens next?
- When should I expect another shipment or charge?

### 2. Reinforce Progress
Help the customer stay consistent and interpret what they are experiencing.

Tactics:
- routine stacking
- missed-day recovery language
- expectation setting
- progress education
- usage reminders
- proactive FAQ content
- product-specific education

The goal is resilient adherence, not perfection.

### 3. Cement the Habit
Before the next major rebill, reinforce why the customer should continue.

Tactics:
- progress validation
- objective or subjective proof
- inventory / usage checks
- cadence controls
- plan adjustments
- value reminders
- milestone recognition

## Behavior Over Calendar

A lifecycle journey should not rely only on “Day 5 email” logic.

Useful behavior signals include:
- module or content completion
- product usage check-ins
- subscription changes
- skipped orders
- account logins
- engagement with education
- offer clicks
- review submission
- support interactions

When possible, trigger communication based on what the customer did or did not do.

## Readiness Score Concept

A useful model can track whether the customer has completed the behaviors that indicate successful onboarding:

- product understood
- dosage / usage confirmed
- expectation window understood
- routine established
- subscription timing understood
- subscription controls known
- progress checkpoint completed

This can roll into a “completely onboarded” or readiness state that powers personalization and measurement.

## What To Measure

Do not judge onboarding on opens and clicks alone.

Better outcomes:
- first rebill completion
- second rebill completion
- early cancellation
- skip / pause behavior
- order-date changes
- onboarding completion
- product usage confidence
- downstream retention
- LTV

## Principle

A good onboarding journey does not simply welcome the customer. It prepares them to succeed with the product and makes the next purchase feel expected rather than surprising.
