# Acquisition Lifecycle

## Objective

Turn interested prospects into high-quality customers without training them to wait for discounts or optimizing only for attributed channel revenue.

## Core Journey

**Capture → Qualify intent → Educate → Prove relevance → Resolve objections → Convert → Hand off into onboarding**

## Segmentation Principles

Segment based on what brought the lead in and what they are trying to solve, not only how long they have been on the list.

Useful inputs include:

- lead source
- product or need-state interest
- quiz or zero-party data
- engagement level
- offer exposure
- email / SMS consent
- site behavior
- prior purchase history

## Messaging Framework

Acquisition messaging should answer four questions:

1. Why should I care?
2. Why this product?
3. Why should I believe it?
4. Why should I act now?

## Testing Philosophy

Campaigns should be used as a learning engine for evergreen lifecycle.

Every test should define:

- the customer question
- the hypothesis
- the variable being tested
- the success criteria
- the decision the result will drive

The goal is not test volume. The goal is to produce learning that changes what we do next.

## Channels

Email and SMS should work together as a coordinated system rather than separate calendars. Other channels can be layered in when they improve the customer journey or reduce friction.

## Handoff to Onboarding

Acquisition should not optimize conversion in isolation. The handoff should preserve:

- the customer's need-state
- the promise or message that drove conversion
- the offer or product configuration
- the source and engagement context

That information should inform the post-purchase experience.
