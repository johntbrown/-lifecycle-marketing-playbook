# Contact Pressure and Channel Orchestration

## Thesis

Email and SMS should work as one lifecycle system, not two independent calendars.

The objective is to increase useful customer contact while minimizing fatigue, redundancy, and attribution confusion.

## Core Questions

Before increasing cadence, know:
- how many messages a person receives across all programs
- how contact pressure differs by lifecycle state
- whether campaign and flow messages overlap
- whether SMS adds incremental conversion or simply takes credit for demand created elsewhere

## Measure Pressure At The Person Level

Track distribution, not just averages.

Useful views:
- messages per person in Day 0–7
- messages per person in Day 0–14
- messages per person in Day 0–30
- median and high-percentile pressure
- Email vs SMS contribution
- flow vs campaign mix

## Channel Role

Email is well suited for:
- education
- proof
- deeper storytelling
- product comparison
- value explanation

SMS is best reserved for moments where immediacy matters:
- high-intent behavior
- deadline / expiration
- billing reminder
- account action
- failed payment
- concise reactivation prompt

## Incrementality

A good first question is:

> Does Email + SMS outperform Email-only enough to justify the additional pressure?

Use a clean holdout when possible.

## Principle

More touches are not automatically better. The right goal is coordinated pressure: the right message, on the right channel, at the right moment, without making the customer feel surrounded.
