# Lead Segmentation and Dynamic Content

## Thesis

Lead nurture should reflect what brought the person in, what they are trying to solve, and what they have done since capture.

Time since signup is useful, but it should not be the only organizing principle.

## Core Lead Dimensions

Segment by:
- acquisition source
- product interest
- lead magnet / quiz response
- stated goal or need-state
- site behavior
- email / SMS engagement
- offer exposure
- conversion stage

## Example Lead States

### New Lead
Recently captured, little additional behavior.

Messaging:
- orient to the brand
- clarify the problem / product fit
- introduce core proof

### High-Intent Lead
Recent product browse, quiz completion, repeated site visit, or checkout activity.

Messaging:
- product-specific proof
- objection handling
- urgency when appropriate
- lower-friction path to purchase

### Educating Lead
Engaged with content but not commercial pages.

Messaging:
- education
- authority
- problem framing
- proof of mechanism

### Offer-Responsive Lead
Repeated engagement with promotional content.

Messaging:
- value framing
- offer clarity
- avoid unnecessary discount escalation

### Dormant Lead
No recent engagement.

Messaging:
- new reason to care
- stronger relevance
- re-permission or suppression logic

## Dynamic Content

Dynamic content allows one journey to behave like multiple journeys.

Useful variables:
- product interest
- problem / goal
- first name
- stated preference
- acquisition source
- prior content engagement
- geographic or retail availability where relevant

Example:
A customer interested in one product should not receive the same hero, proof point, or CTA as someone who entered through a different health need or product category.

## Principle

The goal is not personalization for novelty. The goal is to remove irrelevant information and increase message-to-intent fit.
