# Case Study: Building a First-Rebill Protection System

> Portfolio-safe example. Company-specific data and names are intentionally omitted.

## The Problem

A subscription business was seeing a disproportionate amount of churn around the first rebill. The obvious reaction would have been to add more save offers at cancellation.

The better question was:

> What is happening before the cancellation request that makes the next order feel wrong?

## Diagnosis

The early-cycle experience had several weaknesses:
- customers did not always understand the expected timeline to value
- usage consistency varied
- shipment cadence did not always match consumption
- billing could feel surprising
- subscription controls were not surfaced early enough

The issue was not one broken message. It was customer readiness and subscription fit.

## Strategy

Build a protection layer before the rebill:

1. Clarify expected product usage and progress.
2. Reinforce the habit before the customer evaluates value.
3. Surface billing timing before the charge.
4. Give customers skip, pause, date-change, or right-sizing paths before cancel becomes the only obvious option.
5. Route customers with unresolved product or support issues into the appropriate help path.

## Measurement

Primary outcomes:
- successful first rebill
- early cancellation
- skip / pause / frequency changes

Guardrails:
- support contacts
- refunds
- dunning
- downstream retention

## Lesson

The biggest retention opportunity often exists before the cancellation flow. A save system should begin with readiness and fit, not discounts.
